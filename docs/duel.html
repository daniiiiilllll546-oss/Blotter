THE CUSTOMER'S REQUEST, GIVEN TO ALL FIVE, WORD FOR WORD
I want a bot for Solana. The idea: when SOL drops hard in a short time but the bigger trend is still going up, buy the dip, and take profit fairly quickly on the bounce. If the bigger trend is down, do the opposite: short the sharp rallies. Don't trade when the market is flat and boring. Trades should last a few hours at most. Keep the risk sensible, I don't want it blown up in a day.
Since Sep 30 the desk puts every bot on at least 2% risk and a 2–5× leverage floor, so the risk each model picked is now overridden. Each model worked in a fresh session with no memory of anything else, read the same contract docs, and had to pass the same checker. All five trade SOL on 15-minute candles with a $1,000 paper wallet and face the same midnight cut as every other bot: once a day old, a bot must be profitable over its last 24 hours or it is removed.

How it read the request

Measures the bigger trend with a slow 2-day average, and checks its direction before the move so the dip itself can't flip it. In an uptrend it buys a drop of about 3.4× normal volatility within 2 hours, but only once price has turned back up. In a downtrend it shorts the mirror image.

Takes profit when half the drop is won back, or leaves after 3 hours. Sits out when the trend is too weak or volatility is below its daily norm. Risks 1% per trade and stops trading for the day after 3 losses.

Notes from reading the code
  • Follows every part of the brief: trend direction, both sides, flat filter, a hard 3-hour limit, and a daily loss brake.
  • Chose the lowest risk per trade (1%), before the desk raised everyone to 2%+.
  • Very picky. It made 1 trade in 3 simulated days, and under the midnight rule a day without profit, including a day with no trades, gets it cut.

How it read the request

Calls a trend when a 36-candle average sits at least a third of a volatility unit from a 120-candle average, and that slow average has been moving the same way over the last hour. Quiet markets and extreme candles are skipped.

Entry takes two steps. First it notes a sharp pullback, then it waits up to two candles for price to win back a quarter of a volatility unit, with at least 0.5% of room left to the 14-candle average. It leaves at that average, when the trend fails, or after 2 hours.

Notes from reading the code
  • Covers every part of the brief: both sides, a flat filter, a confirmed recovery before entry and a hard time limit, checked from both the candle count and the clock.
  • Also skips extreme candles, which none of the other four do.
  • Its first version crashed the desk's checker on every call (it used this, which the desk doesn't provide). One fix round and it passed.
  • Two-step entry plus a minimum bounce makes it picky: 3 trades in 3 simulated days.
