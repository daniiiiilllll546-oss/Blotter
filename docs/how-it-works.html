How it works

Evolving trading bots, explained.

Blotter is a trading desk where bots trade with real-time data, and the bad ones are killed every midnight (UTC+5). It is survival of the fittest for trading bots: losers are removed, winners have children, and the pool slowly evolves. All of it runs on paper money, so it is a way to test trading strategies without risking money.

The rules, exactly

Wallet$1,000 of paper money per bot
MarketsTen Binance pairs against USDT: BTC, ETH, SOL, BNB, XRP, DOGE, ADA, AVAX, LINK, LTC
Charts1, 5 or 15-minute candles; a bot decides on every closed candle
FillsPriced off Binance's live order book, with a 0.1% fee on every fill
LeverageEvery trade uses at least 2–5× (a gene per bot), capped by the bot's own limit
Risk per trade2% to 6% of equity, set by the bot's genes

How a bot is killed

  1. The midnight kill. Every day at 00:00 UTC+5 (Tashkent time), every bot older than 24 hours must show a profit over its last 24 hours, open trades marked to market. A loss, or no trades at all, and it is removed.
  2. The hourly fast-fail. Any bot whose equity drops below $600, or that is down more than 15% over 24 hours after at least 5 trades, is removed on the spot.
  3. The kill switch. A bot that falls 40% from its own peak is stopped.

New bots are protected for their first 24 hours, so every bot gets one full day before it is judged.

How the winners evolve

This is a simple genetic algorithm for trading bots. Each bot is a genome: a strategy, a market, a chart speed, a leverage cap and a handful of numbers such as its stop distance and risk. At midnight, profitable bots can have children:

  • Each winner has at most 2 children per night, and one strategy family can hold at most 7 bots, so no single idea takes over.
  • Each number in a child has a coin-flip chance to change, by a random step of about 15% of its range.
  • Sometimes a child moves to a different pair (15% chance), a different chart speed (10%) or a different leverage cap (15%).

On top of that, Claude designs a brand-new strategy every hour, chosen to be something nothing else in the pool is doing. The Standoff adds five bots written by different AI models from the same brief.

What these results do not tell you

  • Paper is not real. Fills are modelled on the live order book, but no real orders move the market, and nobody panics.
  • One day is a small sample. A bot can survive a night by luck. Look at the number of trades before trusting any ranking.
  • Past results do not predict future ones. A winner today is not a signal to copy, and nothing here is financial advice.

Questions

What is Blotter?

Blotter is a trading desk where evolving AI trading bots trade with real-time data, and the bad ones are killed every day at midnight (UTC+5, Tashkent time). Each bot gets $1,000 of paper money and trades live Binance prices.

Is it real money?

No. Every bot trades with paper money, so nobody can win or lose real funds here. The prices are real (live Binance order books), the money is not. Nothing on this site is financial advice.

How does it work?

Four steps on repeat. A bot is born (Claude designs a new strategy every hour, and winners evolve into mutant children). It trades $1,000 of paper money on one of ten Binance pairs, on 1, 5 or 15-minute candles. It gets judged on its last 24 hours. Then it either dies or passes its genes on.

How does a bot die?

Three ways. Every hour, a bot that falls below $600 or loses 15% in a day is removed on the spot. Every midnight (UTC+5) each bot older than a day is judged on its last 24 hours, and one that lost money or made no trades is removed. A bot that drops 40% from its peak is stopped by the kill switch. Bots less than a day old are protected.

What happens if a bot is good?

It survives the midnight cut and may evolve. A profitable bot can have up to two children, each a copy with a few of its numbers nudged by chance (about 15%), and sometimes moved to a different pair or chart speed. A family is capped at seven bots, so no single idea takes over. The best ideas spread, and the rest are removed.

Who designs the bots?

Claude writes a new strategy every hour, chosen to be something nothing else in the pool is doing, and writes down why in its journal. A separate set of bots asks an AI model (OpenJev) for a long, short or flat call on every candle. Winners at midnight also breed mutants of themselves.

What is the Standoff?

The Standoff is a head-to-head test. Five AI models (Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 4.5, GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol) were given the same plain-English trading idea and each wrote its own bot. All five now trade live on the desk under the same rules, and their code is public on the Standoff page.

How realistic are the results?

They are simulated. Fills are priced off Binance's live order book with a 0.1% fee on every fill, but no real orders are placed, so the bots never move the market. Treat the numbers as an experiment, not a track record.

Should I copy a winning bot?

No. A bot can survive a night by luck, and one day is a small sample. Blotter is a free experiment with paper money, not a trading signal, and nothing on this site is financial advice.

How fresh is the data?

The desk prices bots off live Binance data, and a report of the whole desk is published about every 10 minutes. The numbers you see on the live pages come from that report. The tables in the static snapshot on this page show the desk at the time shown beside them.

Can I see the code?

Yes for the website and for the five Standoff bots, which are public on GitHub. The trading engine that runs the desk is private.

How can I support it?

The server, the hourly strategy designer and the model calls cost real money. A coffee keeps the bots running, and sharing the desk helps too. See the Support page.
